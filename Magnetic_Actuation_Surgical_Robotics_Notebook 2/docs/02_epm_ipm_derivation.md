# 2. EPM–IPM wrench and Jacobian derivation

This chapter reconstructs the supplied Appendix A in an implementation-safe form.

## 2.1 Geometry and convention map

The source paper defines

$$
\mathbf p^{P}=\mathbf p_E-\mathbf p_I.
$$

This notebook instead uses the conventional source-to-receiver vector

$$
\mathbf r=\mathbf p_I-\mathbf p_E=-\mathbf p^{P}.
$$

Because the dipole-field tensor $D=3\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^T-I$ is even under $\mathbf r\mapsto-\mathbf r$, the field equation is unaffected. The force is odd and its sign changes. Always document whether a formula reports the wrench **on the IPM** or **on the EPM**.

## 2.2 Wrench

For the wrench acting on the IPM,

$$
\mathbf w_m(\mathbf r,\hat{\mathbf e}_E,\hat{\mathbf e}_I)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf F_m\\[2mm]
\boldsymbol\tau_m
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^6.
$$

Using $\lambda=\mu_0m_Em_I/(4\pi r^3)$,

$$
\boldsymbol\tau_m
=\lambda\;\hat{\mathbf e}_I\times D\hat{\mathbf e}_E,
\qquad
D=3\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^T-I_3.
$$

The corresponding force is the expression derived in Chapter 1. The source paper uses the symbol $\tau_m$ for the complete six-component vector; in controller code, `w_m` prevents confusion with torque alone.

## 2.3 Differentiate the wrench before inserting robot kinematics

Treat the wrench as a function of three independent geometric arguments:

$$
\mathbf w_m=\mathbf w_m(\mathbf r,\hat{\mathbf e}_E,\hat{\mathbf e}_I).
$$

The total derivative is

$$
\dot{\mathbf w}_m
=G_r\dot{\mathbf r}+G_E\dot{\hat{\mathbf e}}_E+G_I\dot{\hat{\mathbf e}}_I,
$$

where

$$
G_r=\frac{\partial\mathbf w_m}{\partial\mathbf r},\quad
G_E=\frac{\partial\mathbf w_m}{\partial\hat{\mathbf e}_E},\quad
G_I=\frac{\partial\mathbf w_m}{\partial\hat{\mathbf e}_I}.
$$

Since $\dot{\mathbf r}=\dot{\mathbf p}_I-\dot{\mathbf p}_E$,

$$
\dot{\mathbf w}_m
=
\begin{bmatrix}-G_r&G_E\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot{\mathbf p}_E\\\dot{\hat{\mathbf e}}_E\end{bmatrix}
+
\begin{bmatrix}G_r&G_I\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot{\mathbf p}_I\\\dot{\hat{\mathbf e}}_I\end{bmatrix}.
$$

This line exposes every sign. If the paper's $\mathbf p^P=\mathbf p_E-\mathbf p_I$ is used instead, the position blocks reverse. The orientation blocks do not automatically acquire the same sign.

## 2.4 Unit magnet-axis kinematics

For a body-fixed unit vector $\hat{\mathbf e}$ with spatial angular velocity $\boldsymbol\omega$,

$$
\dot{\hat{\mathbf e}}
=\boldsymbol\omega\times\hat{\mathbf e}
=-[\hat{\mathbf e}]_\times\boldsymbol\omega
=[\hat{\mathbf e}]_\times^T\boldsymbol\omega.
$$

Define the six-dimensional spatial twist $\mathbf v=[\dot{\mathbf p}^T\;\boldsymbol\omega^T]^T$. Then

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{\mathbf p}\\
\dot{\hat{\mathbf e}}
\end{bmatrix}
=M(\hat{\mathbf e})\mathbf v,
\qquad
M(\hat{\mathbf e})=
\begin{bmatrix}
I_3&0\\
0&[\hat{\mathbf e}]_\times^T
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

This $M$ is not a rigid-body Jacobian. It only maps a pose twist into position and magnet-axis rates.

## 2.5 Insert external and internal kinematics

Let the EPM pose twist be generated by the manipulator Jacobian:

$$
\mathbf v_E=J_R(\mathbf q)\dot{\mathbf q}.
$$

Let $\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}$ denote the internal-device generalized velocity and $J_I(\boldsymbol\chi)$ map it to the IPM twist:

$$
\mathbf v_I=J_I(\boldsymbol\chi)\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}.
$$

The wrench rate becomes

$$
\boxed{
\dot{\mathbf w}_m
=J_q\dot{\mathbf q}+J_\chi\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}}
$$

with

$$
J_q=
\begin{bmatrix}-G_r&G_E\end{bmatrix}
M(\hat{\mathbf e}_E)J_R,
$$

$$
J_\chi=
\begin{bmatrix}G_r&G_I\end{bmatrix}
M(\hat{\mathbf e}_I)J_I.
$$

These are **wrench Jacobians**. Their rows have different units: force rows are N per coordinate and torque rows are N·m per coordinate. Singular values and condition numbers are not physically meaningful unless the rows and columns are scaled.

### Mapping back to the paper

Pittiglio *et al.* write $\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}=J\dot{\mathbf q}$ for their selected end-effector convention and present compact blocks $J_q$ and $J_x$. The placement of a leading minus sign depends on their relative-position and internal angular-state convention. The safe implementation rule is:

1. write the relative vector explicitly;
2. differentiate its definition;
3. derive unit-axis kinematics from $\boldsymbol\omega\times\hat{\mathbf e}$;
4. compare the analytic matrix with central finite differences at several non-symmetric poses.

Notebook 02 performs this check.

## 2.6 Dynamic coupling

For a levitated or soft-tethered capsule, a reduced internal model may be written

$$
B(\boldsymbol\chi)\ddot{\boldsymbol\chi}
+C(\boldsymbol\chi,\dot{\boldsymbol\chi})\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}
+G(\boldsymbol\chi)
+\mathbf d(\boldsymbol\chi,\dot{\boldsymbol\chi})
=S^T\mathbf w_m,
$$

where $S$ selects the wrench components that enter the chosen generalized coordinates and $\mathbf d$ collects tether, contact, drag, and unmodelled forces.

The 2019 paper uses a dynamic force/wrench target and then converts the desired wrench rate to the external robot joint rate. In generic notation,

$$
\dot{\mathbf q}
=J_q^\dagger
\left(dot{\mathbf w}_d+K_w(\mathbf w_d-\mathbf w_m)-J_\chi\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}\right).
$$

The feed-forward term $-J_\chi\dot{\boldsymbol\chi}$ compensates for wrench change caused by internal motion even if the EPM were fixed. This is the conceptual advance over a purely static field-to-pose map.

## 2.7 Source-formula checks

### Apparent cross-term typo

The printed appendix appears to contain

$$
(\hat{\mathbf e}_I^TZ\hat{\mathbf e}_I)I_3
$$

inside the force matrix. The energy-gradient derivation requires

$$
(\hat{\mathbf e}_I^TZ\hat{\mathbf e}_E)I_3.
$$

The first version generally fails an independent numerical energy-gradient check; the second passes. Treat this as an apparent typographical error rather than a new physical model.

### Why finite differences are part of the derivation

A derivation test is not merely a software test. It provides independent evidence that:

- the receiver/source force direction is consistent;
- the unit-vector derivative has the correct sign;
- the matrix dimensions agree;
- the analytic Jacobian predicts the actual local wrench change;
- the chosen rotational perturbation matches the robot Jacobian convention.

Use several random nonsingular poses. Axis-aligned magnets can accidentally hide sign or cross-term errors.
